## Interview framing

- Target: OCI IC3 ownership of a bounded platform component.
  - `IC3 baseline` is what must land in every phase.
  - `IC4 stretch` changes the architecture only when a stronger requirement earns the cost.
  - These are preparation labels, not Oracle's official rubric.
- Evidence: [ranked bank](2.sd.md#1infra-plus).
  - Prior supplemental bank and preparation addition.
  - This is a proposed design, not a claim about OCI internals or a reported accepted answer.
- Why this is hard
  - Naive: append events to a single file and return success when write() returns.
  - First break: the leader dies before replicated durability, or a consumer repeats an external effect after processing but before committing its offset.
  - Second break: adding partitions moves a key, so records for one key now sit in two partitions with no order between them.
  - Minimum primitives
    - Ordered append-only partitions.
    - Replicated acknowledgment policy.
    - Producer retry identity.
    - Consumer assignment and contiguous progress tracking.
- 🗣 “The log orders durable records; the consumer owns what those records do outside the log.”
- Opening script
  - “I will design an ordered partitioned log with replay and explicit acknowledgment semantics.”
  - “I will clarify the ordering key, retention, and the failure set that an acknowledged event must survive.”
  - “Each partition will have one leader and replicated followers.”
  - “The deep dive is retrying a timed-out append and committing consumer progress without skipping unfinished records.”
  - “I will close with retention, rebalances, and the boundary of exactly-once processing.”

## Requirements — IC3 baseline

- Numerical targets are interview assumptions unless an adjacent primary source states otherwise.

| Question | Assumed answer | Why it changes the design |
|---|---|---|
| Ordering scope? | Per partition; same key stays in one partition | No global total order |
| Failure tolerance? | One broker failure with replication factor 3 | Acknowledgment and election policy matter |
| Consumer behavior? | Independent groups with replay | Store progress per group and partition |
| Retention? | 7 days time retention baseline | Consumers can fall behind and lose replay access |
| Exactly once where? | Kafka-like transactions only inside supported log operations | External effects need destination cooperation |

- Functional scope
  - Append records with keys and producer identity.
  - Read by partition offset.
  - Replicate partitions and elect leaders.
  - Track consumer-group assignment and progress.
  - Retain segments and support replay within retention.

- Targets below are interview assumptions, not product SLAs.

| Requirement | Target | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| Append latency | p99 < 20 ms regional assumption | Batching and replicated acknowledgments |
| Availability | 99.9% target under 1 broker loss | 3 replicas and clean leader election |
| Durability | 0 acknowledged loss under stated surviving-replica assumptions | acks=all with minimum ISR 2; storage contract explicit |
| Retention | 7 days baseline | Segment retention policy and capacity admission |
| Record size | Maximum 1 MB | Producer/broker validation |
| Consumer recovery | Target < 30 s after session failure | Group timeout and reassignment |

- Invariants
  - Each partition exposes records in increasing offset order.
  - A producer retry identity cannot append the same accepted sequence twice within its supported epoch/session contract.
  - Consumers only observe the visibility boundary promised by the read mode.
  - Committed consumer progress never skips unfinished lower offsets.
  - Retention loss is reported explicitly rather than silently resetting a consumer.
- Non-goals
  - Global ordering across partitions.
  - Exactly-once arbitrary external side effects.
  - Unlimited replay after retention expires.

## Capacity and consequence — IC3 baseline

- All workload values below are sizing assumptions.
- Ingest
  - Assume `500,000 records/s × 1 KB = 500 MB/s` logical ingress.
  - Decision: batch appends and distribute partition leaders across brokers.
- Retention
  - `500 MB/s × 86,400 × 7 = 302.4 TB` logical seven-day data.
  - Replication factor 3 gives `907.2 TB` before indexes and filesystem overhead.
  - Decision: compression and tiered storage are cost decisions, not optional hand-waving.
- Replica traffic
  - Two follower copies add about `1 GB/s` aggregate leader-to-follower traffic before protocol overhead.
  - Decision: size network and disk jointly under one-broker loss and replica catch-up.
- Consumers
  - Four full-read groups add roughly `2 GB/s` aggregate uncompressed payload delivery.
  - Decision: model egress separately from ingress and isolate slow consumers.
- Partitions
  - If a benchmark supports `10 MB/s` logical writes per partition for this latency target, ingress alone needs at least `50` partitions.
  - Decision: choose extra measured headroom and consumer parallelism without presenting that benchmark as a product constant.

## Architecture and flow — IC3 baseline

```text
Producer -> partition leader -> follower A
                    |        -> follower B
                    |
             append segments + offset index
                    |
           visibility boundary -> consumer group
                                      |
                              destination effect
                                      |
                              contiguous offset commit

Controller quorum -> partition leaders / replica assignment
Group coordinator -> consumer assignment epochs
```

- Partition leader
  - Buys ordered append and batching.
  - Pays a throughput ceiling for one hot partition.
- Replicas
  - Buy survival of the declared broker failure set.
  - Pay network, disk, and catch-up IO.
- Controller and group coordinator
  - Own topology and assignment epochs.
  - Pay coordination and rebalance cost.
- Consumer
  - Owns effect completion and contiguous progress.
  - Pays destination idempotency and replay handling.

→ Map the stable key to a partition using the declared partitioning version.  
→ Send producer ID, epoch, sequence, and record batch.  
→ Leader appends and replicates according to the acknowledgment policy.  
→ Return offsets after the required replication boundary is met.  
→ Assigned consumer reads visible records and commits destination effects.  
→ Commit only the next offset after the contiguous completed prefix.  

## API and data model — IC3 baseline

- Proposed API contracts.
  - Authenticate the tenant from credentials before looking up any resource.
  - Retain idempotency records for the documented retry horizon.

| Surface | Contract | Idempotency and conflicts |
|---|---|---|
| Append(topic, partition, producer, epoch, sequence, batch) | Returns base/last offsets after requested acknowledgment | Retry identity deduplicates within producer protocol; invalid sequence or stale epoch rejected |
| Fetch(topic, partition, offset, maxBytes, isolation) | Returns records and visibility metadata | Offset below retained start returns explicit out-of-range |
| JoinGroup(group, member) | Assignment and generation | Stale member generation cannot commit group progress |
| CommitOffset(group, partition, nextOffset, generation) | Persists next unread position | Reject stale assignment; client must supply contiguous progress |
| Describe(topic/partition) | Leader, retained start, end and configuration | Metadata can become stale; retry after leader change |

| State | Key | Fields and invariant |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | (topic, partition, baseOffset) | Framed records with length and checksum |
| Sparse index | (partition, baseOffset, relativeOffset) | Byte position; rebuildable from verified frames |
| Producer state | (producerId, epoch, partition) | Last accepted sequence and retry result |
| Replica state | (partition, brokerId) | Log end and in-sync status |
| Group progress | (groupId, partition) | Committed next offset and assignment generation |
| Transaction state | (transactionalId, producerEpoch) | Supported log transaction status and visibility markers |

- Reference DDL
  - Keys and uniqueness match the state table above and the [companion schema](../../1sde/1.backend/2.rest102.md#platform-6).
  - Records live in log segments on broker disks; only topic and partition metadata, replica membership, consumer group progress, and retention policy are relational.

```sql
CREATE TABLE topics (
  tenant_id text NOT NULL, topic_id text NOT NULL, name text NOT NULL,
  partition_count integer NOT NULL, replication_factor integer NOT NULL,
  min_in_sync_replicas integer NOT NULL,
  retention_ms bigint NOT NULL, retention_bytes bigint,          -- NULL = no size bound
  cleanup_policy text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'delete',
  tombstone_retention_ms bigint,                                 -- compaction only
  version bigint NOT NULL,                                       -- PATCH precondition
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, topic_id),
  UNIQUE (tenant_id, name),
  CHECK (partition_count >= 1),
  CHECK (replication_factor >= min_in_sync_replicas AND min_in_sync_replicas >= 1),
  CHECK (retention_ms > 0),
  CHECK (cleanup_policy IN ('delete', 'compact', 'compact,delete'))
);
CREATE TABLE partitions (
  tenant_id text NOT NULL, topic_id text NOT NULL, partition integer NOT NULL,
  leader_broker_id text,                                         -- NULL while an election is pending
  leader_epoch bigint NOT NULL,                                  -- fences stale leaders on append and replica fetch
  retained_start_offset bigint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,               -- advances on segment deletion
  high_watermark bigint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,                      -- controller's reported view, not the leader's log
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, topic_id, partition),
  FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id, topic_id) REFERENCES topics,
  CHECK (partition >= 0),
  CHECK (retained_start_offset <= high_watermark)
);
CREATE INDEX partitions_by_leader ON partitions (leader_broker_id, tenant_id, topic_id, partition);
CREATE TABLE partition_replicas (
  tenant_id text NOT NULL, topic_id text NOT NULL, partition integer NOT NULL,
  broker_id text NOT NULL,
  in_sync boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
  log_end_offset bigint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,                      -- last reported by the broker; may lag
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, topic_id, partition, broker_id),
  FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id, topic_id, partition) REFERENCES partitions,
  CHECK (log_end_offset >= 0)
);
CREATE INDEX replicas_by_broker ON partition_replicas (broker_id) WHERE in_sync;
CREATE TABLE consumer_groups (
  tenant_id text NOT NULL, group_id text NOT NULL,
  generation bigint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, protocol text NOT NULL,
  state text NOT NULL, coordinator_broker_id text,
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, group_id),
  CHECK (state IN ('EMPTY', 'REBALANCING', 'STABLE', 'DEAD'))
);
CREATE TABLE group_offsets (
  tenant_id text NOT NULL, group_id text NOT NULL,
  topic_id text NOT NULL, partition integer NOT NULL,
  next_offset bigint NOT NULL,                                   -- next record to process, not last fetched
  group_generation bigint NOT NULL,                              -- commit rejected when below the group's generation
  committed_at timestamptz NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, group_id, topic_id, partition),
  FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id, group_id) REFERENCES consumer_groups,
  FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id, topic_id, partition) REFERENCES partitions,
  CHECK (next_offset >= 0)
);
CREATE INDEX offsets_by_partition ON group_offsets (tenant_id, topic_id, partition, next_offset);
```

- What the DDL does not capture
  - Segments, sparse indexes, and record bytes live in broker log files; producer state is a per-partition snapshot rebuilt from verified frames, and transaction state lives in the coordinator's internal compacted log.
  - `high_watermark` and `log_end_offset` are reported views for lag and retention alarms; the partition leader's log is the source of truth for what is committed.
  - Deviation from the companion and the state table
    - `tenant_id` is added to every key because quotas and retention budgets are enforced per tenant.
    - `Replica state` becomes the controller's ISR view in `partition_replicas`, standing in for the controller quorum's metadata log.

## Append, replication, and retry boundaries — IC3 baseline

- Append storage
  - Frame each record or batch with length and checksum.
  - Track the active file position incrementally.
  - A sparse offset index maps logical offsets to nearby byte positions.
  - Recovery scans verified frames and truncates an incomplete tail.
  - Indexes are rebuildable
  - Committed data is not reconstructed from an index alone.
- Kafka-like acknowledgment policy
  - `acks=all` waits for the current in-sync replica set according to the broker protocol.
  - `min.insync.replicas=2` is an admission threshold for that acknowledgment mode.
  - It is not the same algorithm as a Raft majority commit.
  - With replication factor 3, losing one replica can leave two eligible replicas and continued writes.
  - Disable unclean leader election when preserving acknowledged records takes priority over availability.
- Storage defeater
  - Replication into operating-system page caches is not the same as an fsync on every append.
  - State the chosen broker/storage durability contract and correlated power-loss assumptions.
  - A guarantee against one broker loss does not imply survival of every replica losing volatile state together.
- Producer retries
  - An append timeout is ambiguous.
  - Retry with the same producer epoch and sequence under the idempotent producer protocol.
  - A new producer session or a new business request is not automatically deduplicated against all history.
  - Keep a business event ID if duplicate intent can originate outside that protocol.
- Visibility
  - The high watermark bounds the replicated prefix visible to ordinary consumers.
  - Transactional `read_committed` may stop earlier at the last stable offset while transactions remain open.

## Consumer progress, ordering, and retention — IC3 baseline

- Group ownership
  - One logical member owns a partition within a group assignment generation.
  - A paused former member can still have in-flight external requests.
  - Coordinator fencing protects group commits, not arbitrary destination effects.
- Effect then progress
  - Commit a destination effect before advancing the consumer offset.
  - A crash between them causes replay.
  - Deduplicate by event identity with the destination effect in one transaction.
  - Kafka transactions can coordinate supported Kafka records and offsets, not an unrelated database automatically.
- Parallel handling
  - Track completed offsets and advance only through the contiguous prefix.
  - If offsets 10 and 12 finished but 11 did not, commit next offset 11.
  - Committing 13 would skip unfinished record 11 after restart.
- Partition growth
  - Increasing partition count can remap a key under modulo hashing.
  - Old and new partitions do not share a total order.
  - If key order matters, version routing and drain/fence old producers before switching that key.
- Retention and compaction
  - Time retention deletes eligible old segments regardless of a slow consumer's progress.
  - Expose earliest retained offset and alarm before consumer lag reaches the boundary.
  - Log compaction retains a latest-value view eventually, subject to its cleaner and tombstone policies.
  - Compaction is not immediate deletion or an exact per-key TTL.
- Poison records
  - Bound retries and use an explicit quarantine policy.
  - Preserve the original topic, partition, offset, and failure reason.
  - Skipping a record changes the application contract and must be visible.

## Failure, operations, and security

| Failure | Blast radius | Detection | Degraded behavior | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leader dies after append reply | One partition | Leader heartbeat | Clean eligible replica takes over under stated durability assumptions | Retry ambiguous records with producer identity |
| ISR drops below minimum | Affected partitions | ISR count and append errors | Reject durable writes | Recover replicas before reopening |
| Consumer dies after effect | One group partition | Session timeout | New member replays | Destination deduplication returns prior result |
| Slow consumer crosses retention | One group | Lag versus earliest retained offset | Explicit out-of-range | Restore from archive or perform declared resnapshot |
| Hot partition | One key range | Per-partition latency and bytes | Backpressure that partition | Change key distribution only with an ordering migration |
| Controller quorum unavailable | Leader elections and topic changes | Controller health | Existing partition leaders keep serving appends and fetches | Restore controller quorum; pending elections complete |
| Group coordinator unavailable | Commits and rebalances for its groups | Commit errors | Consumers keep processing on the last assignment; commits retry | Coordinator moves to another broker; replay from last commit |
| Broker disk full | Partitions on that broker | Disk usage and append errors | Follower drops from ISR; leader rejects writes if minimum ISR is unmet | Expand or trim retention, then catch up |
| Poison record | One partition of one group | Retry count on the record | Quarantine with topic, partition, offset, and reason | Fix the consumer; replay quarantined offsets |

- Observe
  - Under-replicated partitions and ISR changes.
  - Append latency and replica catch-up IO.
  - Consumer lag in both offsets and time.
  - Oldest open transaction and retained-start movement.
- Security
  - Encrypt broker traffic and restrict retention-policy changes.
  - Audit deletion and retention changes as control-plane operations.
- Retry discipline
  - Producer retries reuse the same producer epoch and sequence, with backoff and jitter, within the delivery timeout.
  - A stale-epoch or out-of-sequence rejection is a corrective action: re-initialize the producer, do not retry blindly.
  - Consumers retry a failed effect a bounded number of times, then quarantine the record and leave the offset uncommitted.
  - An offset-commit rejection for a stale generation means rejoin and re-read the assignment, never a forced commit.
- Tenant isolation
  - Enforce tenant byte-rate and storage quotas.
  - Authorize topic access and consumer-group operations independently.
  - Place critical tenants' partitions in separate broker cells so one tenant's hot partition cannot fill a shared disk.
  - Budget retention per tenant so one tenant's backlog cannot push another's segments off disk.
  - A tenant's credentials receive `404` on another tenant's topics, not `403`, to avoid existence leaks.
- Rollout and rollback safety
  - Preserve replica availability during rolling upgrades.
  - Version record schemas and test old consumers before adding required fields.
  - Bump the inter-broker protocol and log format only after every broker runs the new version, so a rollback never meets an unreadable segment.
  - Canary one broker, watch under-replicated partitions and append latency, then continue.
  - Reassign replicas gradually to avoid saturating replication bandwidth.

## Alternatives and IC4 stretch

| Option | What it is | Strength | Weakness | Choose when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Replicated event log | Ordered retained partitions | Replay and independent consumers | Retention and partition operations | Baseline event backbone |
| Task queue | Work delivery with acknowledgments | Simple competing workers | Replay and ordering may be limited | Independent jobs rather than event history |
| Database outbox plus relay | Business state and event intent | Atomic source transaction | Relay lag and duplicate delivery | Events originate with a database mutation |
| Object-storage archive | Immutable batch history | Low retention cost | Higher retrieval latency | Long replay horizon beyond hot storage |

- Stronger requirement
  - Kafka-to-Kafka exactly-once processing uses supported transactions and read_committed consumers.
  - External destinations still require their own atomic effect boundary.
- At 10× load
  - Add partition cells and tier cold segments to object storage.
  - Model replica recovery bandwidth before selecting broker count.
- Cost and blast radius
  - Separate critical topics from noisy bulk ingestion.
  - Enforce retention budgets so one tenant cannot exhaust the shared disk.
- Migration
  - Version producer routing and drain old key ownership before repartitioning ordered streams.
- Weakest assumption
  - The selected failure domains and storage contract match the stated durability guarantee.
- Over-engineering warning
  - Do not promise global ordering or exactly-once external effects by merely enabling a producer setting.

## Level signal — IC3 baseline vs IC4 stretch

| Phase | IC3 must land | IC4 adds | Separating signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | Define partition ordering and replay horizon | Negotiate destination transaction boundary | No unqualified exactly-once claim |
| Capacity | Calculate replication and retention bytes | Tiering and recovery bandwidth | Includes cost beyond ingress |
| Correctness | Explain ISR and producer identity | Transaction visibility and epoch migration | Does not confuse Kafka with Raft |
| Operations | Commit contiguous progress | Repartition without breaking key order | Recovery preserves semantics |
| Architecture | Leader, replicas, controller, coordinator, consumer, one trace | Cost of each split and static stability under controller loss | Every component has a mechanism and a price |
| Wrap | Recap with retention and hot partitions as limits | Names the storage-contract assumption as the weakest | Identifies the flaw before the interviewer does |

## Interview pushback and validation

- Q1: Does acks=all mean a majority vote?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - No
    - It follows the current ISR acknowledgment rule with a separate minimum-ISR admission setting.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Contrast broker replication with a consensus metadata controller instead of conflating them.
- Q2: Producer timed out after a successful append?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - Retry the same supported producer identity and sequence.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Use a business event ID for duplicate intent across producer sessions.
- Q3: Consumer wrote the database and crashed before offset commit?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - It replays, so deduplicate with the database effect in one transaction.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Use an outbox or transactional destination pattern for additional emitted events.
- Q4: Can parallel processing commit the highest completed offset?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - Only if every lower offset in the assigned range completed too.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Track contiguous progress and bound in-flight gaps to control replay cost.
- Q5 (premise reversal): One global order is required?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - One partition creates a global serialization bottleneck.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Negotiate whether only related keys need order before paying for a sequencer and its availability limit.
- Q6: Can adding partitions preserve order automatically?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - No
    - A key can move while old records remain in its former partition.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Use a routing-version migration with producer fencing and a drain boundary.
- Q7 (premise reversal): Skip the log and have producers call consumer APIs directly; why pay for retention?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - Direct calls couple producer availability to every consumer and lose replay, so a consumer bug or outage drops events.
    - The log decouples them and turns a consumer outage into lag instead of loss.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Choose direct calls or a task queue when there is one consumer, no replay need, and an idempotent destination.
    - Keep an outbox at the producer so the transport can change later without losing events.

- Validation scenarios
  - Kill a leader before and after acknowledgment and verify the stated failure contract.
  - Retry timed-out producer sequences and verify one accepted log append.
  - Drop ISR below the configured minimum and reject writes.
  - Complete consumer offsets out of order and verify only contiguous progress is committed.
  - Crash after destination commit and replay without a second effect.
  - Increase partition count under keyed traffic using the declared routing migration.
