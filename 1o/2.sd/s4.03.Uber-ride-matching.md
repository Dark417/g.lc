## Interview framing

- Target: OCI IC3 ownership of a bounded platform component.
  - `IC3 baseline` is what must land in every phase.
  - `IC4 stretch` adds architecture changes under stronger constraints.
  - These are preparation labels, not Oracle's official rubric.
- Evidence: first-hand report attribution `1p3a ×2` is retained in [the bank](2.sd.md#2business).
  - Two collected reports name this prompt, so it is the most likely business prompt in the loop.
  - The architecture below is a proposed interview answer.
  - It does not claim to reproduce Uber's implementation or an interviewer's accepted answer.
- OCI framing for a business prompt
  - Control plane: trip and driver state, quotas, pricing configuration, tenant onboarding.
  - Data plane: location ingest, geo index, live-location fan-out.
  - Static stability: an in-progress trip must finish even if the trip database is unavailable.
  - Blast radius: one region or city cell fails alone.
- Why this is hard
  - Naive: a `drivers` table with `lat`, `lon`, a `WHERE distance < r` query, and "assign the first row".
  - First break: hundreds of thousands of location writes per second and a full-table distance scan on every request.
  - Second break: two riders receive the same driver because "nearby and available in the cache" is not an atomic assignment.
  - Third break: reordered or replayed location updates move a driver backward, and a driver that lost connectivity still looks available.
  - Minimum primitives
    - A cell-indexed in-memory latest-location store with freshness expiry.
    - Session and sequence fencing on every location update.
    - An offer with a deadline and a deterministic identity.
    - One conditional transaction that claims the trip and the driver together.
    - A versioned trip state machine with actor-specific transitions.
- 🗣 “The geo index only nominates candidates; the trip transaction is the only thing that assigns a driver.”
- Opening script
  - “I will split this into a high-write location data plane and a low-write, transactional trip control plane.”
  - “First I confirm the scale of online drivers, the matching policy, and what must keep working when the trip store is down.”
  - “Then I show that location ingest is a memory and fan-out problem while matching is a correctness problem at a much lower rate.”
  - “The architecture is a fenced location ingest into a cell index, a per-region matcher that issues timed offers, and a trip service whose accept transaction is the single point of assignment.”
  - “The deep dives are the geo index with freshness and the offer-accept race across two riders and one driver.”
  - “I will close with degradation when the trip store is unavailable, tenant isolation, and where batched matching changes the design.”

## Requirements — IC3 baseline

- Numerical targets are interview assumptions unless an adjacent primary source states otherwise.

| Question | Assumed answer | Why it changes the design |
|---|---|---|
| Scale? | `1M` drivers online globally at peak, `100k` in the busiest metro, `25M` trips/day | Location ingest is the high-write path; matching is two orders of magnitude lower |
| Location update interval? | Every `4 s` while online, `1 s` while on a trip | Fixes write rate, freshness window, and fan-out rate |
| Matching policy? | Nearest-ETA driver, one offer at a time, driver may decline | Sequential offers with a deadline; batched assignment is an IC4 stretch |
| Who is the tenant? | A city or fleet-operator partition in one deployment | Tenant key on every row; matching never crosses tenants |
| Consistency of assignment? | Exactly one driver per trip and one active trip per driver | Needs a transactional claim, not a cache flag |
| What survives a trip-store outage? | In-progress trips continue; new requests fail fast | Device holds trip state; ingest and fan-out do not depend on the trip store |
| Pricing and payment in scope? | Quote before request; settlement after completion, out of the matching path | Quote has a bounded lifetime; payment is an idempotent downstream operation |

- Functional scope
  - Ingest driver locations and keep a fresh nearest-driver index.
  - Quote a ride, accept a ride request, and match it to one available driver.
  - Offer, accept, decline, and time out with bounded retries.
  - Drive the trip through a state machine with actor-specific transitions.
  - Show the assigned driver's live location to the rider only.
  - Cancel with explicit state-dependent rules and settle after completion.

| Requirement | Target | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| Location write rate | `250k/s` global, `25k/s` per busiest region | In-memory latest-location store sharded by region and cell; no relational write per update |
| Location freshness | Index entry expires after `30 s` without an update | TTL on the latest-location entry; offer re-checks freshness |
| Time to first offer | p95 < `3 s` from request | Cell cover query plus ETA on top `10` candidates only |
| Time to assignment | p95 < `60 s`; `NO_DRIVERS` after `5` rounds or `90 s` | Offer deadline `15 s`; bounded rounds per trip |
| Assignment correctness | `0` double-assigned drivers or trips | One conditional transaction plus a partial unique index on active trips per driver |
| Trip-state availability | `99.95%` for transitions | Stateless trip API; replicated relational store per region |
| Live-location latency to rider | p95 < `2 s` | Pub/sub keyed by trip; websocket gateway sharded by trip |
| Static stability | In-progress trips complete during a `30 min` trip-store outage | Device-held trip state; completion events queue and replay |
| Location history retention | `30 d`, async | Append to a log and object storage, never on the ingest hot path |

- Invariants
  - A driver holds at most one active trip at any time.
  - A trip has at most one assigned driver, chosen by exactly one committed accept transaction.
  - A location update is applied only if its `(session_epoch, seq)` is newer than the stored value.
  - A candidate returned by the geo index is never treated as assigned until the trip transaction commits.
  - A trip transition requires the expected version and an actor allowed to perform it.
  - A rider sees only the location of the driver assigned to that rider's active trip.
- Non-goals
  - Route planning and turn-by-turn navigation; the routing service is a dependency with an ETA API.
  - Surge-pricing models; the quote service is an input, not this design.
  - Ride pooling and multi-stop trips in the first release.
  - Fraud detection and driver onboarding.

## Capacity and consequence — IC3 baseline

- Location ingest
  - `1M` online drivers updating every `4 s` is `250k updates/s` globally.
  - The busiest metro at `100k` drivers is `25k updates/s`.
  - Each update is about `120 B`, so ingest is roughly `30 MB/s` globally, which is trivial network but not a trivial write rate.
  - Decision: latest location lives in an in-memory store sharded by region and cell.
    - A relational row update per location is the wrong tool at this rate.
    - The store is a projection that can be rebuilt from live updates within one freshness window.
- Latest-location state size
  - `1M` entries at about `200 B` with index membership is about `200 MB`.
  - Decision: one region's state fits in memory on a few nodes with a replica.
    - Shard by cell prefix so a hot metro does not sit on one node.
- Location history
  - `250k/s × 120 B ≈ 30 MB/s`, about `2.6 TB/day` raw, roughly `500 GB/day` compressed at an assumed `5×`.
  - `30 d` retention is on the order of `15 TB`.
  - Decision: history is an async append to a log and then object storage.
    - It is never on the ingest acknowledgment path.
    - Partition by driver and time bucket for trip replay and disputes.
- Ride requests and matching
  - `25M trips/day ≈ 290 requests/s` average; a `4×` peak is about `1.2k/s` globally and about `120/s` in the busiest metro.
  - One match covers `10–40` cells and filters `50–300` candidates by freshness and availability.
  - Decision: ETA comes from the routing service only for the top `10` candidates by straight-line distance.
    - Routing calls stay near `12k/s` at global peak instead of `300k/s`.
    - Cache ETAs per cell pair for a few seconds where precision allows.
- Offer rounds
  - A `15 s` offer deadline with an assumed average of `1.5` rounds gives a typical assignment in `15–25 s`.
  - `5` rounds bound the worst case near `75–90 s` before `NO_DRIVERS`.
  - Decision: rounds and deadline are per-tenant configuration, not code constants.
- Trip state
  - `25M trips/day` at about `1 KB` per trip is `25 GB/day`, on the order of `9 TB/year`.
  - Active trips at any time are a few hundred thousand rows.
  - Decision: the hot relational store holds active and recent trips per region.
    - Archive completed trips by tenant and month to a cheaper store.
- Live-location fan-out
  - An assumed `60%` of `1M` online drivers on a trip is about `600k` active trips at peak.
  - Each active trip has one rider subscriber at `1 update/s`, so fan-out is about `600k messages/s`.
  - Decision: fan-out is a pub/sub topic per trip behind websocket gateways.
    - The rider-facing rate is capped at `1/s` regardless of driver update rate.

## Architecture and flow — IC3 baseline

```text
[data plane: locations]
Driver app -> location gateway (authn, fence epoch/seq) -> latest-location store (in-memory, per region, cell-indexed)
                                   |                              |
                                   +-> location log (async) -> object storage (history, 30 d)
                                   +-> pub/sub topic per trip -> websocket gateway -> rider app (assigned driver only)

[control plane: trips]
Rider app -> trip API (authn, tenant, quota) -> trip store (quotes, trips, drivers, offers, outbox)
                 |                                    |
                 +-> matcher (per region/cell lease) -+-> geo query -> candidates -> routing ETA (top N)
                                                      +-> offer (deadline 15 s) -> push -> driver app
                                                      +-> accept = one conditional transaction
                 |
                 +-> outbox relay -> settlement (idempotent by trip), notifications, analytics
```

- Location gateway owns admission and fencing of driver updates.
  - Mechanism bought: authenticates the driver session, rejects stale `(session_epoch, seq)`, and forwards only monotonic updates.
  - Cost paid: every update carries a session identity, and a restarted app must obtain a new epoch before its updates count.
- Latest-location store owns the nearest-driver index.
  - Mechanism bought: an in-memory map keyed by driver plus a per-cell membership set, with a `30 s` freshness TTL.
  - Cost paid: it is a projection with no durability promise; a node loss forgets drivers until their next update arrives.
- Matcher owns candidate selection and offers for one region or cell lease.
  - Mechanism bought: a single logical writer per trip during matching, so offers are sequential and rounds are bounded.
  - Cost paid: a lease holder can crash mid-round; the offer deadline and lease expiry are what bound the stall.
- Trip API and trip store own authoritative trip and driver state.
  - Mechanism bought: a relational store with versioned rows, a partial unique index on active trips per driver, and an outbox in the same transaction.
  - Cost paid: single-region writes; a store outage stops new assignments even though ingest and in-progress trips continue.
- Routing service is a dependency, not a component of this design.
  - Mechanism bought: ETA ranking that beats straight-line distance in cities.
  - Cost paid: a synchronous call on the matching path; fall back to haversine ranking when it is slow.
- Pub/sub and websocket gateway own live-location delivery.
  - Mechanism bought: one topic per active trip so authorization is a topic subscription check.
  - Cost paid: connection state on the gateway; a gateway loss forces reconnect and resubscribe.

→ Rider calls `POST /ride-quotes`; the quote is priced server-side with a bounded `expires_at`.  
→ Rider calls `POST /ride-requests` with the quote and an `Idempotency-Key`; the trip row is created in `MATCHING` and returns `202`.  
→ The matcher for the pickup cell takes the trip, computes the cell cover for the search radius, and reads candidates from the latest-location store.  
→ Candidates are filtered by freshness and by the availability projection, then the top `10` by straight-line distance are ranked by routing ETA.  
→ The matcher inserts one offer for the best driver with a `15 s` deadline and a deterministic `offer_id`, then pushes it to the driver app.  
→ The driver calls `POST /trip-offers/{id}/acceptances`; the transaction verifies the offer, the trip version, and the driver's `AVAILABLE` state, then claims both.  
→ The commit writes an outbox event; the rider is notified and subscribed to the trip's location topic.  
→ The driver transitions the trip through `ARRIVING`, `IN_PROGRESS`, and `COMPLETED` with `expectedVersion`; completion emits a settlement event keyed by `trip_id`.  

## API and data model — IC3 baseline

- Paths omit the `/v1` prefix and follow the [shared API conventions](../../1sde/1.backend/2.rest102.md#shared).
  - `I` means `Idempotency-Key` is required.
  - `V` means a strong `If-Match` validator is required.

| Surface | Contract | Idempotency | Conflict/error behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| `POST /ride-quotes` | `{pickup, dropoff, rideType}`; returns `201 {quoteId, amountMinor, currency, expiresAt}` | Read-like; a new quote per call | `422` on unserviceable area |
| `POST /ride-requests` | `{quoteId, pickup, dropoff}`; returns `202 {tripId, state: MATCHING}` | `I` per rider | `409` on an expired quote or an existing active trip for the rider |
| `PUT /drivers/{id}/location` | `{sessionEpoch, seq, lat, lon, capturedAt}`; returns `200 {acceptedSeq}` | Naturally idempotent by `(epoch, seq)` | Stale `(epoch, seq)` returns `200` with the higher accepted seq and is not applied |
| `PUT /drivers/{id}/availability` | `{state: AVAILABLE\|OFFLINE}`; returns `200 driver` | `V` | `409` when an active trip owns the driver |
| `POST /trip-offers/{id}/acceptances` | `{expectedTripVersion}`; returns `200 trip` | `I` per driver | `409 OFFER_LOST` when the offer expired, the trip moved on, or the driver is no longer `AVAILABLE` |
| `POST /trip-offers/{id}/declines` | `{reason}`; returns `204` | Idempotent by offer state | `409` if already accepted |
| `POST /trips/{id}/transitions` | `{expectedVersion, event}`; returns `200 trip` | `I` per actor | `409` on version mismatch or an event the actor may not send |
| `POST /trips/{id}/cancellations` | `{reason, expectedVersion}`; returns `200` or `202 cancellation` | `I` | `409` if the trip is `IN_PROGRESS` or terminal; fee rule depends on state |
| `GET /trips/{id}` | Authorized trip view | Read | `404` for a trip the caller does not own or drive |
| `GET /trips/{id}/driver-location` | Latest assigned-driver location, or a stream subscription token | Read | `404` unless the caller is the rider or driver of an active trip |

| State | Key | Fields/invariant |
|---|---|---|
| Quote | `(tenant_id, quote_id)` | Rider, amount in minor units, currency, route hash, `expires_at` |
| Driver | `(tenant_id, driver_id)` | State `OFFLINE\|AVAILABLE\|BUSY`, version, current `session_epoch` |
| Trip | `(tenant_id, trip_id)` | Rider, quote, driver, state, version, pickup, dropoff, `created_at`; partial unique index on `(tenant_id, driver_id)` while active |
| Offer | `(tenant_id, offer_id)` | Trip, driver, round, `expires_at`, state `OFFERED\|ACCEPTED\|DECLINED\|EXPIRED\|LOST` |
| Latest location | `(tenant_id, driver_id)` in memory | `session_epoch`, `seq`, `lat`, `lon`, `received_at`, cell; TTL `30 s` |
| Cell membership | `(tenant_id, region, cell)` in memory | Set of driver IDs with freshness metadata |
| Location history | `(tenant_id, driver_id, time_bucket)` | Append-only points under retention |
| Idempotency record | `(tenant_id, caller_id, operation, intent_key)` | Request hash and stored response; retained beyond the retry horizon |
| Outbox | `(tenant_id, event_id)` | Aggregate, version, payload; inserted in the same transaction as the state change |

- Reference DDL (the companion holds the full schema; this adds the offer fields the matcher needs)

```sql
CREATE TABLE trip_offers (
  tenant_id  text NOT NULL, offer_id text NOT NULL,      -- offer_id = hash(trip_id, driver_id, round)
  trip_id    text NOT NULL, driver_id text NOT NULL,
  round      integer NOT NULL, expires_at timestamptz NOT NULL,
  state      text NOT NULL,                              -- OFFERED|ACCEPTED|DECLINED|EXPIRED|LOST
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, offer_id),
  UNIQUE (tenant_id, trip_id, round),                    -- one offer per round
  FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id, trip_id) REFERENCES trips,
  FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id, driver_id) REFERENCES ride_drivers
);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX driver_one_trip ON trips (tenant_id, driver_id)
  WHERE state IN ('ASSIGNED','ARRIVING','IN_PROGRESS');
```

- Conventions
  - `tenant_id` and the actor come from authenticated context, never from the body.
  - `expectedVersion` in the body is a domain precondition on the trip, so its failure is `409`, not `412`.
  - A stale location update returns `200` rather than an error so that a driver app never retries a rejected point.
  - Money is minor units plus currency; timestamps carry offsets.

## Location ingest and geo index — IC3 baseline

- Cell choice
  - Geohash precision `6` is a cell of about `1.2 km × 0.6 km` at the equator; that is the assumed index resolution.
  - S2 level `13` or H3 resolution `8` are the same order of magnitude and avoid geohash's edge-length distortion at high latitudes.
  - The interview claim is "a hierarchical cell index at roughly `1 km`"; the library is a knob.
  - Cell size trades index selectivity against the number of cells a search radius touches.
- Index structure
  - `driver -> (epoch, seq, lat, lon, cell, received_at)` is the latest-location map.
  - `cell -> set(driver)` is the membership index.
  - A location update that changes cell removes the driver from the old cell and adds it to the new one under the driver's lock.
  - Freshness uses `received_at` at the server, not the device's `capturedAt`, so device clock skew cannot keep a stale entry alive.
- Search
  - Compute the set of cells that intersect the circle of the search radius around the pickup.
  - Query all intersecting cells; a fixed `9`-neighbor query is only correct when the radius is smaller than one cell.
  - Filter candidates by `received_at` within `30 s` and by the availability projection.
  - Rank by straight-line distance, then re-rank the top `10` by routing ETA.
  - Widen the radius per round, for example `2 km`, `4 km`, `8 km`, until the tenant's cap.
- Fencing

```text
APPLY_LOCATION(driver, update):
    lock driver entry
    if update.epoch < entry.epoch: return accepted_seq = entry.seq          # old session
    if update.epoch == entry.epoch and update.seq <= entry.seq: return entry.seq  # reordered or replayed
    move driver between cells if the cell changed
    entry = (update.epoch, update.seq, update.lat, update.lon, cell, now())
    publish to trip topic if the driver is on an active trip (rate-capped to 1/s)
    return entry.seq
```

  - `session_epoch` is issued by the availability endpoint when the driver goes online; an app restart gets a new, higher epoch.
  - `seq` increases per session on the device; a lost response is retried with the same `seq`.
  - Without the fence, two gateways receiving updates out of order can move a driver backward and the matcher offers a trip to a driver who has already left.
- Consistency boundary
  - The latest-location store is eventually consistent and lossy by design.
  - It answers "who is probably near here right now", never "who is assigned".
  - Availability in the index is a projection of the driver row; the trip transaction re-reads the driver row.
- Concrete values
  - `30 s` TTL is about `7` missed updates at a `4 s` interval; shorter TTLs drop drivers on brief signal loss, longer TTLs offer trips to drivers who are gone.
  - Per-region store shards by cell prefix; the busiest metro spreads across several nodes so one node does not carry `25k/s`.
- Exact failure
  - A store node is lost.
  - Its drivers vanish from the index for up to one update interval, then reappear as updates arrive.
  - Matching in those cells widens its radius for a few seconds; nothing is assigned incorrectly because assignment never reads the index as truth.

## Matching, offers, and the trip state machine — IC3 baseline

```text
MATCHING --accept commits--> ASSIGNED --driver arrives--> ARRIVING --pickup--> IN_PROGRESS --dropoff--> COMPLETED
MATCHING --rounds exhausted--> NO_DRIVERS
MATCHING|ASSIGNED|ARRIVING --rider or driver cancel--> CANCELLED (fee rule depends on the state it left)
```

- Offer lifecycle
  - The matcher holds a lease on the trip's pickup cell or region, so one logical writer issues offers for a trip.
  - `offer_id = hash(trip_id, driver_id, round)` and `UNIQUE (tenant_id, trip_id, round)` make an offer insert idempotent.
  - A matcher retry after a crash reads the existing offer for the round instead of issuing a second one.
  - The offer carries `expires_at = now + 15 s`; the push to the driver is best effort and the driver app can also poll its pending offer.
  - On decline or expiry, the matcher moves to the next candidate and increments the round.
  - After `5` rounds or `90 s`, the trip transitions to `NO_DRIVERS` with the same expected-version discipline.
- Accept transaction

```text
ACCEPT(tenant, offer_id, driver, expected_trip_version):
    transaction:
        offer = select trip_offers for update where offer_id
        require offer.driver_id == driver and offer.state == OFFERED and now() < offer.expires_at
        trip  = select trips for update where trip_id = offer.trip_id
        require trip.state == MATCHING and trip.version == expected_trip_version
        drv   = select ride_drivers for update where driver_id
        require drv.state == AVAILABLE
        update trips set state = ASSIGNED, driver_id = driver, version = version + 1
        update ride_drivers set state = BUSY, version = version + 1
        update trip_offers set state = ACCEPTED where offer_id
        insert outbox (TripAssigned, trip_id, version)
    on any require failure: return 409 OFFER_LOST
    on unique violation of driver_one_trip: return 409 OFFER_LOST
```

  - Lock order is offer, then trip, then driver, in every code path, so two accepts cannot deadlock.
  - The partial unique index is the second line of defense: if a bug ever offers one driver two trips, the second commit fails instead of double-booking.
  - The driver's `Idempotency-Key` replays the stored `200` on a retried accept, so a lost response does not surface as `OFFER_LOST`.
- Concurrency cases
  - Two riders, one driver: only when broadcast offers are enabled can both offers exist; the first commit wins and the second returns `409 OFFER_LOST`.
  - Rider cancels while an offer is outstanding: cancellation moves the trip to `CANCELLED` with a version bump, so the accept fails its version check.
  - Driver goes `OFFLINE` while holding an offer: the availability endpoint is `V`-guarded and the accept re-reads `AVAILABLE` under lock.
  - Offer expires during the accept: the deadline check runs inside the transaction using the database clock.
- Transition rules
  - Each event names the actors allowed to send it; `ARRIVING`, `IN_PROGRESS`, and `COMPLETED` are driver-only, `CANCELLED` is rider or driver with different fee outcomes.
  - Every transition is `UPDATE ... WHERE version = expected` and inserts an outbox row in the same transaction.
  - A retried transition with the same `Idempotency-Key` replays the stored response.
- Completion and settlement
  - `COMPLETED` writes a `TripCompleted` outbox event with the final fare inputs.
  - The settlement service charges once per `trip_id` with its own idempotent record and compensates on failure.
  - Payment failure never reopens the trip; it creates a receivable to resolve out of band.
- Static stability
  - The driver app holds the trip state and the next allowed transitions locally.
  - If the trip store is unavailable, the driver continues the ride and the app queues `IN_PROGRESS` and `COMPLETED` with their idempotency keys.
  - Location ingest and the rider's live map keep working because they never touch the trip store.
  - New ride requests fail fast with `503` rather than queueing indefinitely, because a rider waiting for a driver that cannot be assigned is worse than an honest error.
- Exact failure
  - The matcher crashes after inserting an offer and before pushing it.
  - The lease expires, a new matcher reads the round's existing offer, and re-pushes it if time remains.
  - If the offer already expired, the new matcher moves to the next round; the driver app ignores an expired offer by `expires_at`.

## Failure, operations, and security

| Failure | Blast radius | Detection | Degraded behavior | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Latest-location store node lost | Drivers in that shard's cells for one update interval | Shard health; candidate count drop per cell | Matching widens radius; no incorrect assignment | Entries rebuild from live updates within `30 s` |
| Location updates reordered or replayed | One driver | Rejected-seq counter | Stale update ignored, `200` returned | None needed; fence is the correction |
| Driver loses connectivity while `AVAILABLE` | One driver | TTL expiry | Driver drops out of candidates after `30 s`; an outstanding offer expires | Driver reconnects with a new epoch |
| Matcher crash mid-round | Trips in one lease | Lease expiry; oldest `MATCHING` age | Round stalls up to the lease TTL | New lease holder resumes from the persisted offer |
| Push notification lost | One offer | Driver app poll; offer expiry | Driver polls pending offers; expiry moves on | Next round |
| Two accepts race for one driver | One driver, two trips | `409 OFFER_LOST` rate | Second accept rejected | Matcher offers the losing trip elsewhere |
| Routing/ETA service slow | Match quality in one region | Call latency, timeout rate | Rank by straight-line distance; ETA cache | Re-enable ETA ranking when latency recovers |
| Trip store unavailable | New requests and transitions in one region | Health check; error rate | In-progress trips continue on device; ingest and live map work; new requests get `503` | Fail over the replica; devices replay queued transitions by idempotency key |
| Settlement service down | Payments, not trips | Outbox lag | Trips complete; charges queue | Outbox relay retries; reconcile receivables |
| Region boundary trip | One trip crossing regions | Driver cell outside home region | Home region keeps trip ownership; location routes by driver, not by cell | Handoff only for a new trip after completion |

- Observe
  - Location updates per second, rejected-seq rate, and per-cell candidate counts.
  - Time to first offer, time to assignment, and rounds per trip by tenant.
  - Offer expiry rate and `OFFER_LOST` rate.
  - Oldest `MATCHING` trip age per region.
  - Outbox lag and settlement retry depth.
  - Live-location delivery latency and gateway connection churn.
- Retry discipline
  - Driver location updates retry the same `(epoch, seq)` with a short deadline, then skip to the newest point.
  - Accept, transition, and cancellation retries reuse the same `Idempotency-Key` until a terminal answer arrives.
  - The matcher never retries an offer insert with a new `offer_id`; it re-reads the round.
  - Routing calls have a hard timeout of a few hundred milliseconds and a haversine fallback, not a retry loop.
- Tenant isolation
  - Every key starts with `tenant_id`; the cell index is namespaced by tenant so a search never returns another operator's drivers.
  - Per-tenant quotas cap ride requests per second and online drivers.
  - A caller receives `404`, not `403`, for another tenant's trip or driver IDs.
  - Matching configuration, such as radius steps and offer deadline, is per tenant.
- Rollout and rollback safety
  - Trip-state schema changes follow expand, migrate, contract; a new transition rule must accept trips created by the previous version.
  - Canary the matcher by region; a bad ranking change affects one region's match quality, not assignment correctness.
  - Location gateway deploys are rolling; the in-memory store is rebuilt from live updates, so a node restart costs one update interval.
  - Driver-app and API versions overlap; an unknown event from a newer app returns `400`, never a silent state change.

## Alternatives and IC4 stretch

| Option | What it is | Strength | Weakness | Choose when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Geohash cells | Base-32 prefix cells in an ordered key space | Simple; prefix range scans on any sorted store | Cell shape distorts by latitude; neighbor logic at edges | Baseline with a sorted KV or in-memory sets |
| S2 or H3 cells | Hierarchical spherical cells (quad or hex) | Uniform-ish area; region cover for a radius is a library call | One more dependency; hex has no exact parent containment | Global service where latitude distortion matters |
| Redis `GEOSEARCH` | Sorted-set geohash with radius queries | Ready-made radius query with no index code to write | No per-member TTL, so freshness needs a side structure; single-key hot spot per region; still not an assignment | Small deployments or a prototype |
| PostGIS or R-tree | Spatial index inside the database | Exact geometry; joins with trip data | Cannot absorb `250k` writes/s | Low-rate fleets or analytics |
| Sequential offers | One driver at a time with a deadline | No double-offer race; simple fairness | Slower assignment under declines | Baseline |
| Broadcast offers | Offer to top `k` at once | Faster acceptance | `k−1` losers; needs `OFFER_LOST` handling | Low supply or high decline rates |
| Batched assignment | Solve riders-to-drivers per cell every `2–5 s` | Better global ETA; fewer offers | Latency floor; solver complexity | Dense metros at peak |

- IC4 stretch — batched matching
  - Collect `MATCHING` trips per cell for a `2–5 s` window and solve a small assignment problem on ETA.
  - The accept transaction does not change; the batch only decides who gets offered first.
  - Cost: a latency floor per window and a solver that must be bounded per cell.
- IC4 stretch — 10× load
  - `2.5M updates/s` still fits in memory per region; shard cells across more nodes and replicate each shard.
  - Matching at `12k/s` global peak needs ETA caching per cell pair and routing calls capped per region.
  - Move the location log to a partitioned append log with per-region consumers; history stays off the hot path.
  - Split the trip store by region and then by tenant when a single relational store's write rate is the limit.
- IC4 stretch — cost and blast radius
  - The in-memory store and websocket gateways dominate cost; the trip store is small.
  - Cell the deployment per region so a region's matcher, store, and gateway fail together and alone.
  - Degrade by widening radius and dropping ETA ranking before dropping matching entirely.
- IC4 stretch — pooling and region handoff
  - Pooling changes the invariant to one active trip per driver with many riders; the partial unique index becomes a capacity check.
  - Cross-region trips keep ownership in the home region; only new trips after completion move.
- Weakest assumption
  - Driver location updates arrive at `4 s` intervals with independent losses.
  - A carrier outage makes a whole neighborhood stale at once, so the index shows drivers who are all unreachable and every offer expires.
  - Mitigation: treat the offer itself as a liveness probe, shorten the TTL during high expiry rates, and alarm on per-cell offer-expiry spikes.
- Over-engineering warning
  - Do not build a global assignment optimizer before sequential offers with a bounded radius are measured.
  - Do not make the geo index strongly consistent; correctness lives in the trip transaction.
  - Do not call the routing service for every candidate.

## Level signal — IC3 baseline vs IC4 stretch

| Phase | IC3 must land | IC4 adds | Separating signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Online drivers, update interval, one-driver-one-trip invariant, what survives a trip-store outage | Names pooling and batched matching as non-goals and what cutting them buys | Negotiates the matching policy instead of accepting it |
| Estimates | `250k/s` ingest, `1.2k/s` matches, `600k/s` fan-out | Lets the ingest rate rule out the relational store and the match rate cap routing calls | A number changes a decision |
| Architecture | Location data plane versus trip control plane with one trace | States the cost of each split: a lossy projection and a single-region trip store | Every component has a mechanism and a price |
| Deep dive | Cell cover query, `(epoch, seq)` fence, accept transaction with lock order and unique index | Broadcast and batched offers with `OFFER_LOST`; leases and idempotent offer rounds | Reasons across the index and the transaction boundary |
| Operations | Failure table, degraded matching, static stability of in-progress trips | Per-region cells, tenant quotas, rollout of ranking changes | Volunteers degradation and rollout before being asked |
| Wrap | Recap and tradeoff | Names correlated staleness as the weakest assumption first | Identifies the flaw before the interviewer does |

## Interview pushback and validation

- Q1: Why not query drivers within a radius directly from the database?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - `250k` writes/s and a distance scan per request do not fit a relational store.
    - The index is a projection; the database is for assignment.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - PostGIS is right for fleets of thousands, and saying where the crossover lies is the signal.
- Q2: Two riders request at the same time and the nearest driver is the same.
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - Sequential offers per driver mean only one trip holds an offer for that driver at a time.
    - The accept transaction and the partial unique index reject any second claim.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Under broadcast offers, the loser gets `409 OFFER_LOST` and its matcher moves on within the same round.
- Q3: A driver's phone reconnects and replays old location updates.
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - The `(session_epoch, seq)` fence drops anything not newer than the stored value.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Server receipt time drives freshness, so a device clock cannot resurrect a stale entry.
- Q4: The trip database goes down for 20 minutes.
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - In-progress trips continue on the device; ingest and the live map keep working; new requests get `503`.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Queued `COMPLETED` transitions replay by idempotency key, and settlement waits on the outbox rather than on the outage.
- Q5: How do you guarantee an offer is not sent twice after a matcher crash?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - `offer_id = hash(trip_id, driver_id, round)` and the unique round constraint make the insert idempotent.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - The lease holder re-reads the round on takeover and re-pushes only if the deadline has not passed.
- Q6: Now add ride pooling.
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - The one-trip-per-driver invariant becomes a seat-capacity check inside the same transaction.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Matching becomes an insertion problem against an active route, which is where batched assignment earns its cost.
- Q7 (premise reversal): Why push offers at all instead of letting drivers poll for nearby requests and claim one?
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - Driver-pull turns every request into a race among many drivers, so most claims lose and the rider waits on contention rather than on ETA.
    - The claim transaction would still be needed, so pull buys nothing on correctness.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Pull is reasonable for low-density markets with few requests; then the claim is the same accept transaction with a first-come rule and the cell index serves drivers instead of riders.
- Q8: The interviewer asks for live locations of all nearby drivers on the rider's map before the request.
  - **IC3 baseline**
    - Serve a coarse, cell-level snapshot with reduced precision and no driver identity, at a low refresh rate.
  - **IC4 stretch**
    - Precise per-driver positions to arbitrary clients are a privacy and scraping problem; the assigned-driver-only topic stays the only precise feed.
- Validation scenarios
  - Two accepts for one driver within the same second.
  - Rider cancels while an offer is outstanding.
  - Location update with an older epoch after an app restart.
  - Matcher crash between offer insert and push.
  - Trip store unavailable during `IN_PROGRESS`, then `COMPLETED` replayed twice.
  - Search radius larger than one cell at the cell edge.
  - Routing service timeout during ranking.
  - Tenant A token on tenant B trip ID returns `404`.

## Recall card

- “The geo index nominates; the trip transaction assigns.”
- “Fence every location update by session epoch and sequence, and expire it by server time.”
- “One offer per round with a deterministic ID and a deadline; the accept claims trip and driver together under a unique index.”
- “In-progress trips must survive a trip-store outage; new requests should fail fast.”
- “Cover the radius with cells; never assume nine neighbors.”
- Wrap-up script
  - “The design is a fenced location data plane feeding a cell index, a per-region matcher issuing timed idempotent offers, and a transactional trip service with a versioned state machine.”
  - “The key invariant is that a driver has at most one active trip, enforced by one conditional accept transaction and a partial unique index, never by the index.”
  - “The main tradeoff is a lossy in-memory location projection: it absorbs `250k` writes per second and rebuilds itself, in exchange for candidates that are sometimes stale and must be re-verified by the offer.”
  - “My weakest assumption is independent location loss; a correlated carrier outage stales a whole neighborhood, so I would validate offer-expiry alarms and adaptive TTL first.”
