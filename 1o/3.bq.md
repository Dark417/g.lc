# 1P3A Reports — Oracle BQ (deduplicated)

> All behavioral questions moved from [1.coding/0.1p3a.md](1.coding/0.1p3a.md) records, deduplicated, grouped by idea, ranked by how many reports asked the theme. `(#N)` = source record. 八股 / brain teasers stay in 1.coding/0.1p3a.md.
> Each group: 题目 → **Template** (STAR 骨架) → **Critical points** (评分点 + 陷阱)。

Meta (how Oracle runs BQ):

- BQ 出现在几乎每一轮: coding 轮各带 ~2 题 (#1, #6); 专门的全 BQ 轮常见 (#1 R3, #4 R4, #9 VO1 一小时 8-9 题, #10 R3 HM, #11 HM call)
- 要求 STAR 格式回答 (#4)
- HM 轮会 push 真实案例, 流程化答案不过关 (#6); Bartender/Bar Raiser 反而压力可能小于 HM (#6)
- 备 4-5 个核心故事, 一个故事覆盖多组: ①最难项目 (→3/4/8) ②真实冲突 (→1) ③线上故障 (→5) ④需求模糊起步 (→4) ⑤deadline 取舍 (→2); 每个故事按 STAR 写 6-8 句, 带数字

## 1. 冲突 & 难相处的人 — 5 reports, 出现最稳定

- Tell me about a conflict within your team and how you handled it (#4, #5, #6, #7, #8)
- Handling tough people / 应对难相处的人 (#7)

**Template**

- S/T: 和谁 (peer/跨组)、争什么 (具体技术或优先级决策)、为什么双方都有道理、stakes 是什么
- A: ① 先 1:1 私聊弄清对方真实 concern → ② 把分歧转成客观标准 (data / prototype / 成本-风险对比) → ③ 当面讨论收敛, 说清你被说服还是说服了对方 (或各让一步的具体方案)
- R: 决策结果 + 量化效果 + 关系维护 (之后还合作愉快) + 你学到的一条

**Critical points**

- ⚠ #6 实测: 答 "找第三方 / escalate / 用 data 说服 / document + move on" 被 HM 打断 push "你有过真的冲突吗?" — 必须是真冲突: 立场明确对立、有情绪成本; escalate 只能是最后一步且框成 "request a tiebreak", 不能是开场
- 展示直面 (主动发起对话), 不是绕开; "讨论了一下就同意了" 不算冲突
- 你被说服、承认自己错了的版本反而加分 (显 low ego)
- 选 peer/跨组冲突, 避开和自己 manager 的冲突
- tough people: 把难相处归因于压力/目标不一致, 不做人身评价; 讲你怎么建立 working relationship

## 2. Prioritization & deadline — 4 reports

- Prioritization approach (#9, #11)
- 两个 critical tasks 怎么排优先级 (decision-making scenario) (#5)
- 有没有特别紧的 deadline (#1)
- Describe a time when you could not meet a tight deadline (#4)

**Template**

- 排序框架 (说出口): customer/revenue/SLA 影响面 × 紧急度 × 不可逆性 × 依赖阻塞面; 和 stakeholder 对齐而不是自己闷头排
- 行动: 明确 tradeoff → 通知被 deprioritize 的一方并给替代方案 (砍 scope / 换 owner / 定新时间) → 设 checkpoint 防偏
- miss-deadline 版 A: **尽早预警** (一发现风险就说, 不是 deadline 当天) → 给新 estimate + 补救计划 (砍 scope 保核心 / 分阶段交付) → R: 交付结果 + 事后复盘估算为什么错

**Critical points**

- "could not meet deadline" 考的是提前沟通 + ownership, 不是加班英雄主义
- 必须给明确的决策标准, 不能 "我觉得这个重要"
- 被放下的那件事要有下落 (不是消失) — 面试官会追问
- 两个 critical task 场景题: 先问清 "critical 对谁" — 展示先收集信息再决策

## 3. 项目 / ownership / 技术决策 — 5 reports

- 参与过什么复杂的项目 / 复杂项目详细解释 (#1, #7)
- 提出过什么设计优化 / 优化过什么方案 / 对某个系统做过改进 (#1, #3)
- Describe a difficult technical decision you made (#4)
- Leadership and ownership experiences (#5, #7)
- Business impact of the projects (#5)
- Stakeholder communication (#5)
- 工作中遇到的 challenge (#9)
- Have you changed scope (#9)
- What have you done to increase the productivity of the team (#17)
- 相关故事素材 (来自 #1 HM 聊项目): 最难的项目怎么解决; 最骄傲的项目及原因; 怎么 oncall

**Template**

- 主力项目故事: 背景/规模 (QPS、数据量、团队几人) → 你的角色 → 核心难点 → 关键决策: 2-3 个备选方案 + 选择标准 + 为什么不选另外两个 → 量化结果 (latency/成本/错误率 before→after) → 后续影响 (被别的组采用 / 成为标准)
- 准备三个变体同一骨架: 最难 (突出技术深度)、最骄傲 (突出 impact)、优化 (突出 before/after 指标)
- ownership 版: 讲一次超出职责边界的行为 — 主动发现问题、跨组推动、oncall 跟到 root cause 并落防复发

**Critical points**

- 没有 alternatives + tradeoff 的 "决策" 会被追问 "为什么不用 Y"; 先自己讲掉
- 数字是硬通货: 说不出量化结果 = 没有 impact
- business impact 要翻译到钱/用户/SLA, 不停留在技术指标
- changed scope: 展示适应 + 主动重新谈判 scope; 抱怨 = 扣分
- oncall 故事同时喂 #5 (failure) — 复用

## 4. Ambiguity / 信息不足 / 不熟悉问题 — 4 reports

- 在需求不明确的情况下怎么处理 (#3)
- 描述一个在信息非常少的情况下做出决策的场景 (#7)
- 遇到不熟悉的问题、没有充分相关信息, 怎么着手解决 (直接补充)
- Handling ambiguity (#5)
- 注: bank 里 LC 11 那轮也把 "how do you approach an unfamiliar problem" 当软性 probe — 同一主题

**Template**

- ① 收敛问题: 写下已知/未知, 向 stakeholder 问最小关键问题集 (不是要全部信息)
- ② 降低不确定性: 找 proxy — 现有数据、类似系统的先例、timebox 的 spike/prototype
- ③ 带 assumption 行动: 选可逆的小步先走, 把 assumptions 写进 doc, 设验证 checkpoint
- ④ 纠偏: assumption 被证伪时怎么调头 (决策做成可逆的, 所以代价小)
- 不熟悉问题版: 文档/代码/tests → 找 owner 或专家问 15 分钟 → 复述确认 mental model → 小实验验证

**Critical points**

- 核心信号是 bias for action: 不是 "等信息齐了再做", 而是 "用 X 手段在 Y 时间内把不确定性降到能决策"
- assumptions 必须说出口 + 有验证动作, 否则像赌博
- 这题在 coding 轮也会被当软性 probe (碰到没见过的题) — 现场就用同一框架念出来: 复述、举例确认 I/O、说 brute force 再优化

## 5. Failure & customer — 4 reports

- Handling failure (#9, #11)
- Most critical bug you dealt with (#17)
- Customer impact (#9, #11)
- 有没有和客户直接沟通的经验, 怎么解决问题 (#1)

**Template**

- failure: 什么坏了 → customer 实际影响量化 (多少用户 / 多久 / 多少请求) → **先止血** (rollback/mitigate) 再查 root cause → 对内对外的沟通 (主动上报、给 ETA) → blameless postmortem → 机制性防复发 (alert/test/流程)
- customer 沟通: 听懂真实诉求 (往往和字面要求不同) → 翻译成技术方案 + 讲清 tradeoff → 设定预期 (能做什么、什么时候) → 跟进闭环

**Critical points**

- 顺序错误是红旗: 一定 mitigate 先于 root cause
- 主动承认 + 主动上报; 隐瞒或甩锅直接挂
- "学到了什么" 必须是机制 (加了什么 alert/test/checklist), 不是 "以后更仔细"
- Oracle 是企业客户生意 — customer impact 和客户沟通被连问两轮 (#9 #11), OCI 面试官在找 enterprise 客户意识

## 6. Motivation & company fit — 4 reports

- 为什么换工作 / 为什么离开现在的公司 (#7, #9)
- 为什么选 Oracle (#7 — HM 和 Bar Raiser 各问一次)
- 是不是真的愿意接这个 offer (#12 — director 轮直问)
- 什么让你在工作中 motivate (#3)
- 是否愿意做全栈 (前端/后端都接受) (#7)

**Template**

- why switch: 拉力为主 (想做的 scale/领域/成长) + 一句中性推力 (成长空间收敛), 绝不抱怨现雇主
- why Oracle: 具体到 OCI/团队/产品 — 提 JD 里的东西 (cloud infra 的规模、和 AWS 竞争的阶段、能 own 的范围), 加一个人连接点 (用过/研究过某服务)
- motivate: 一个具体例子 (啃下难题的过程 / 用户用上的反馈 / 学到新领域), 收在与该岗位对齐
- 全栈: "yes, and" — 举一次跨栈交付的经历 + 学习意愿; 不设边界

**Critical points**

- why Oracle 在同一 loop 被 HM 和 BR 重复问 (#7) — 两次答案必须一致, 面试官会对答案
- 贬低现雇主 = 红旗
- motivate 别答 "钱/WLB"; 答案要能落到这个组的日常工作上

## 7. AI — 3 reports, 新趋势必准备

- How do you use AI in your daily work? (#4)
- What is your opinion on AI and how do you think it improves software engineering? (#4)
- 怎么用 AI 优化自己的个人项目 (pet project) (#7)
- 对 AI 的看法 (#12 — director 轮)

**Template**

- daily use: 具体工具 + 具体工作流 (boilerplate/测试生成/code review 辅助/查不熟的 API) + 一个量化例子 (某任务从 X 降到 Y) + **验证环节** (AI 输出必 review、跑测试)
- opinion: 平衡论 — 提效在 boilerplate/探索/学习, 边界在架构、权衡、正确性责任仍在工程师; 落点: 工程师的价值往 设计/review/判断 上移
- pet project: 讲一个真实 side project — AI 用在哪个环节、省了什么、你把关了什么

**Critical points**

- "没怎么用" = 落后; "全靠 AI" = 没判断 — 都挂; 答案必须体现 "用得多 + 有质量门槛"
- 必须有具体例子, 面试官在验证你是否真用过
- 主动提 verification/quality gate 是成熟信号

## 1. How OCI runs behavioral

```
Question ─► Situation/Task (15%) ─► Action (60%, "I" not "we") ─► Result + metric + learning (25%)
            └── follow-ups: what first? why that? alternatives? manager satisfied? what changed after?
```

- Interviewer checklist (from Oracle's own pack)
  → S: what happened, who involved, main issue
  → T: what responsibility did _you_ take; assigned or self-initiated
  → A: what first, how did people respond, what next, what alternatives
  → R: end result, manager reaction, did you keep owning it, new responsibilities
- Rules
  → Beginning-middle-end; no generalizations; be ready for 2–3 levels of follow-up
  → Tell it with energy — recruiter said "be engaging and excited"
  → Respect confidentiality — no JPMC internal specifics beyond what's public/resume-level
  → Pick stories where the _value_ is visible, not just the tech
- Key interviewer ("bartender")
  → Outside team; stern; will push on job change, conflict, design justification
  → Mediates your debrief — this round's impression carries weight

## 2. The 10 OCI values — decoded

> Source of the value text: the OCI Interview Prep Pack. Every quoted line below is verbatim from that document; the full text is in [0.req/0.req.md](0.req/0.req.md#req-c5).
> The prep pack says outright that they "will ask questions directly related to these values", so treat this as the published rubric rather than background colour.
> Read the table for recall. Read [§2.1–§2.10](#bq-2-1) for what a passing answer actually has to contain.

### 2.0 How to use this section

- Every value is a **behavior they want evidence of**, not an attitude you can assert.
  - Saying "I always put customers first" scores nothing.
  - Describing a request you refused to take literally, and what it cost you, scores the point.
- Each value has a **cost clause** hidden in its wording.
  - "Customers win every time" implies you gave something up.
  - "A grungy solution now is superior to no solution at all" implies you shipped something you were not proud of.
  - An answer with no cost in it reads as a slogan, and the interviewer will probe until they find the cost or conclude there was none.
- Two values are about **disagreement** and they are not the same.
  - `Earn trust, give trust` is about how you disagree: with data, not rank.
  - `Challenge ideas, champion execution` is about what you do after losing: execute fully anyway.
  - Interviewers routinely ask one and follow up into the other.
- The **key interviewer** round is where value questions concentrate.
  - That interviewer is an outsider to the team and also mediates your debrief.
  - They have no technical stake in your design opinions, so stories must stand up without domain context.
- Note on round 1
  - The recruiter's process email lists "Act now, iterate" directly under the first round.
  - Reading that as the competency assigned to that round is an inference, not a stated fact, but it is a cheap one to prepare for.

### 2.1 Quick table

| # | Value | What it actually means | What they listen for | What sinks the answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Put customers first](#bq-v1) | Serve the real need over the literal request, and over what is convenient for you | A request you did not take at face value, and the cost you absorbed to serve the need behind it | The "customer" is an internal stakeholder and nothing user-visible changed |
| 2 | [Act now, iterate](#bq-v2) | A rough thing shipped beats a perfect thing discussed; bring solutions, not problem statements | A visibly imperfect v1 that shipped, plus the specific v2 that followed | You describe a long design phase, or a v1 that was never actually improved |
| 3 | [Nail the basics](#bq-v3) | Fundamentals before flash; recognise a missing foundation and go fix it | You stopped to fix tests, monitoring, or a data model while feature pressure was on | Foundations you fixed only because someone assigned them to you |
| 4 | [Expect and embrace change](#bq-v4) | Realign fast; do not defend priorities you set last quarter | A priority flip you re-planned inside, including the handoff you wrote | You describe the change as an interruption, or you quietly kept working the old plan |
| 5 | [Innovate together](#bq-v5) | Empathy and inclusion as a working method; other people's ideas make it into the result | Someone else's idea you adopted over your own, named and credited | A collaboration story where every good idea turns out to be yours |
| 6 | [Take risks, remain calm](#bq-v6) | Risk assessed with data; composure during an incident; failure treated as input | A bet you sized before taking, or an incident where your first move was diagnosis | Heroics with no assessment, or a "risk" that had no downside |
| 7 | [Own without ego](#bq-v7) | Admit error first; fix or find an owner; never "not my job" | A mistake you surfaced before anyone caught it, and what changed structurally afterwards | A mistake that was really someone else's, or one with no lasting fix |
| 8 | [Earn trust, give trust](#bq-v8) | Transparency; learn from failure instead of assigning blame; never pull rank | A disagreement settled by evidence, where you name what would have changed your mind | You won because you were senior, or the story has no disagreement left in it |
| 9 | [Take pride in your work](#bq-v9) | Excellence as a standard you hold, including refusing to ship half-done | A quality bar you raised when nobody was checking, and what you said no to | Perfectionism with no delivery, or pride with no standard behind it |
| 10 | [Challenge ideas, champion execution](#bq-v10) | Ask why, test the idea rigorously, own the decision, then drive it fully | You argued hard against a decision, lost, and then delivered it properly | You challenged and then disengaged, or you never challenged anything |

### 2.2 Per-value detail

Each entry below gives the value in their words, the behavior being tested, what a passing answer contains, the follow-ups to expect, the traps, and the story from [§5](#bq-5) that covers it.

<a id="bq-v1"></a>

#### Value 1 — Put customers first

- **Their words**
  - "We exist to satisfy our customers."
  - "We put doing the right thing for customers ahead of doing what they specifically say or ask for."
  - "When faced with a choice between what is easy for us and what is good for customers, customers win every time."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Whether you can tell a stated request apart from the need underneath it.
  - Whether you have ever paid a real cost, in your own time or scope, to serve that need.
  - The second sentence is the whole value: doing what you were asked is the baseline, not the signal.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - A specific request, quoted close to how it arrived.
  - How you found out the real need, and from whom.
  - The thing you built or refused to build as a result.
  - The cost you absorbed: extra scope, a slipped date, an argument you had to have.
  - A customer-visible outcome, with a number where one exists.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "How did you know that was the real need, rather than your own preference?"
  → "What if the customer had insisted on the original request?"
  → Oracle's own published version: "What would you do differently knowing what you know now?"
- **Traps**
  - An answer where serving the customer cost you nothing. They will keep probing until they find the cost or decide there was none.
  - Substituting an internal stakeholder for a customer with no user-visible result.
  - Overriding the customer without evidence, which reads as arrogance rather than service.
- **For a platform role specifically**
  - On an internal platform team your customers are the service teams that consume your API.
  - That reframe is worth saying out loud, because it makes every API-design decision a customer story.
  - Concretely for PKI: a team asks for a longer certificate lifetime, and the real need is that their renewal keeps failing.
  → Giving them the long lifetime is the easy answer and the wrong one.
  ⇒ Fixing renewal serves the need and keeps the blast radius small.
- **Your story**
  - [#3 preview/correct/query APIs](#bq-5): users asked for a query endpoint; the real pain was correcting bad normalized records quickly.
  - Lock the number for corrections or support tickets before the loop.
- 🗣 "They asked for a query endpoint. What they actually needed was to fix a bad record in under a minute, so I built the correction flow first and shipped the query with it."

<a id="bq-v2"></a>

#### Value 2 — Act now, iterate

- **Their words**
  - "We favor action. Notice something that needs fixing? Fix it. See a gap? Fill it."
  - "We recognize that a grungy solution now is superior to no solution at all."
  - "We keep it simple. We don't discuss endlessly, and we are scientific in our approach. We offer solutions, not problem statements."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Bias to action without waiting for permission or for a complete picture.
  - Willingness to ship something you are not proud of, and then actually improve it.
  - "We are scientific" is the counterweight: fast, but measured, not reckless.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - The gap you noticed, and that nobody assigned it to you.
  - How rough the first version genuinely was, said plainly.
  - How fast it shipped, in days rather than a vague "quickly".
  - The specific v2 that followed, which is the half most candidates omit.
  - What you measured to know it worked.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "What was wrong with the first version?"
  → "What would have happened if you had waited and designed it properly?"
  → "When is a grungy solution the wrong call?"
- **Traps**
  - Describing a long design phase and calling it action.
  - A v1 that shipped and was never iterated on, which proves the first half of the value and disproves the second.
  - Recklessness with no measurement, which collides with "scientific in our approach".
  - The strongest version of this answer includes where you would *not* apply it: a signing key, a migration, anything one-way.
- **Your story**
  - [#1 Lambda test-data injector](#bq-5): end-to-end testing was blocked on slow manual record setup.
  - v1 in days, ugly, covering 30+ record types on demand; debugging time down roughly 80%; the team adopted it.
  - Prepare the v2: what you added after the first version, and why.
- 🗣 "Nobody asked me to build it. Testing was blocked, so I shipped an ugly injector in three days and cleaned it up once people were unblocked."

<a id="bq-v3"></a>

#### Value 3 — Nail the basics

- **Their words**
  - "We focus on fundamentals over flash."
  - "We recognize when we don't have the basics in place, and we diligently work to fill the gaps."
  - "We recognize that the path to advanced solutions always runs through the basics."
  - "We make forward progress despite not having complete information or perfect solutions."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Whether you can recognise a missing foundation while feature pressure is on.
  - Whether you went and fixed it rather than routing around it.
  - This is the value most aligned with the job description's operational language: tests, monitoring, runbooks, data models.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - The advanced thing everyone wanted to build.
  - The basic thing that was missing underneath it, named precisely.
  - The breakage that proved it was missing, ideally one that had already happened.
  - The case you made for spending time on foundations, and to whom.
  - What became possible afterwards that was not possible before.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "How did you convince your manager to spend a sprint on that?"
  → "How did you decide it was worth the delay?"
  → "What basics are missing on your current team right now?"
- **Traps**
  - Foundations you fixed because they were assigned to you, which removes the judgment being tested.
  - Gold-plating presented as fundamentals, which collides with "what is currently appropriate".
  - Waiting for complete information, which the value explicitly rejects.
- **For a platform role specifically**
  - The basics for a certificate service are the boring ones: expiry monitoring, renewal that works unattended, an inventory you can query by serial.
  - A team without those has an outage scheduled for whenever the longest certificate expires.
- **Your story**
  - [#4 canonical data model plus validation](#bq-5): 200+ inconsistent vendor attributes causing downstream breakages.
  - Normalized into validated domain objects with a canonical store; 40K records a day stable afterwards.
  - Fix the disputed numbers before the loop: 40K versus 30K, and 200 versus 150 or 170 attributes.
- 🗣 "We kept patching downstream breakages one at a time. The basic thing missing was a validated canonical model, so I built that first and the breakages stopped being a category."

<a id="bq-v4"></a>

#### Value 4 — Expect and embrace change

- **Their words**
  - "We accept change as a given."
  - "We value people who align quickly with current priorities, who have situational awareness, and who are willing to adapt."
  - "We are not limited to priorities we set in the past. We do not hang on to outdated processes and goals, and we promote or accept new ideas fearlessly."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - How fast you realign, and whether you resent it while doing so.
  - Whether you protected the work you were leaving, rather than abandoning it.
  - "Situational awareness" is the quiet part: did you understand *why* the priority changed?
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - The change, and who decided it.
  - Your honest first reaction, briefly. Claiming delight is less credible than saying it stung and you moved anyway.
  - Why the new priority was right, demonstrating you understood the business reason.
  - The handoff you actually produced, in documents or sessions, so the old work was not stranded.
  - How quickly you were productive on the new thing.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "What happened to the work you left behind?"
  → Oracle's own published version: "What do you do when priorities change quickly? Give one example."
  → "Have you ever pushed back on a priority change? What happened?"
- **Traps**
  - Framing the change as an interruption or as management churn.
  - Quietly continuing the old plan, which is the failure mode this value exists to screen out.
  - Total passivity: adapting without ever understanding why is compliance, not situational awareness.
- **Your story**
  - [#7 priority flip](#bq-5): reassigned to the agent system mid-quarter.
  - Prepare two concrete details: what the handoff artifact was, and how long until you shipped something on the new work.
- 🗣 "I had two weeks left on the pipeline work when it got reprioritised. I wrote the handoff the same week so nobody had to reverse-engineer it, and I had the first agent running inside a fortnight."

<a id="bq-v5"></a>

#### Value 5 — Innovate together

- **Their words**
  - "We practice empathy and respect in our interactions."
  - "We challenge our internal biases and learn to be more effective allies."
  - "We seek and celebrate the diverse perspectives and thought leadership that add value to our workplace, teams, and products."
  - "We foster an inclusive environment where collaboration drives innovation."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Whether other people's ideas visibly survive into your results.
  - Whether you actively pull in people who were not in the room.
  - This is the value where interviewers most often hear a monologue about the candidate's own brilliance.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - A named collaborator or team, and what they knew that you did not.
  - A specific idea of theirs you adopted **over** your own, and why theirs was better.
  - How you created the conditions for it: who you invited, what you asked, how you made it safe to disagree with you.
  - The result that would not have existed had you worked alone.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "Whose idea was the final design?"
  → "How do you get input from someone who is not speaking up?"
  → "Tell me about working with someone whose style is very different from yours."
- **Traps**
  - A collaboration story where every good idea turns out to be yours. This is the single most common way to fail this value.
  - Listing meetings attended as evidence of collaboration.
  - Treating inclusion as a policy topic rather than as how you get better answers.
- **Your story**
  - [#2 discrepancy-detection agent system](#bq-5): worked with the vendor-email owners who understood the source data.
  - Pick one concrete thing they told you that changed the design, and say you were wrong about it first.
- 🗣 "I had the routing design wrong because I did not know how the vendor emails were actually formatted. The team that owned them told me, and their version of the extraction step is the one that shipped."

<a id="bq-v6"></a>

#### Value 6 — Take risks, remain calm

- **Their words**
  - "We take risks because they are necessary to our success; not taking risks is the biggest risk of all."
  - "We are logical and data-driven in assessing our risks."
  - "We react to unexpected situations by remaining calm, and then making and executing mitigation plans."
  - "We recognize that learning from our failures is part of our path to success."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Two distinct behaviors that the interviewer may probe separately.
  - Risk-taking: did you size the downside before committing?
  - Composure: in an incident, was your first move diagnosis rather than panic or blame?
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - For the risk half: the safe option you rejected, the downside you sized, and the reversibility of the bet.
  - For the incident half: the first thing you did, and the order you did things in.
  - The mitigation plan as a plan, not as thrashing.
  - What you changed afterwards so the same class of failure could not recur.
  - Numbers: detection time, time to mitigate, blast radius.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "What was the worst case, and how did you bound it?"
  → "What did you do in the first ten minutes?"
  → "What would you do differently now?"
- **Traps**
  - Heroics with no assessment, which fails "logical and data-driven".
  - A "risk" with no real downside, which was not a risk.
  - Mitigating without a durable fix, so the story has no learning in it.
  - Naming a person as the root cause. That collides with `Earn trust, give trust` in the same breath.
- **For a platform role specifically**
  - This is the value the on-call and postmortem language in the job description maps onto.
  - Have one incident where you can narrate the timeline cleanly: detection, hypothesis, disproof, cause, mitigation, guardrail.
- **Your story**
  - [#5 production incident and root cause analysis](#bq-5): cross-service failure surfaced by alerts, traced through monitoring, remediation coordinated.
  - The open gap is the guardrail. Decide what you actually added afterwards, because the follow-up always lands there.
- 🗣 "The alert fired at a service that turned out to be downstream of the real problem. I traced it across services before touching anything, because restarting the noisy one would have destroyed the evidence."

<a id="bq-v7"></a>

#### Value 7 — Own without ego

- **Their words**
  - "We take responsibility for the state of our team, our products, and ourselves."
  - "We are the first to admit when we are wrong."
  - "We welcome all help and feedback, and we recognize and incorporate the best ideas offered."
  - "When we notice a problem, we either fix it ourselves or find another owner. We never say, “That’s not my job.”"
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Whether you surface your own mistakes before someone else finds them.
  - Whether you pick up problems outside your lane instead of routing around them.
  - The "or find another owner" clause matters: ownership includes handing something to the right person, not hoarding it.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - A mistake that was genuinely yours, stated in one sentence without hedging.
  - Who you told, how fast, and whether they already knew. Telling them first is the whole point.
  - What you did in the first hour to contain it.
  - The structural change afterwards, so the fix outlives you.
  - No blame distributed anywhere in the telling.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "Did anyone else already know before you raised it?"
  → "What stopped the same thing happening again?"
  → "Tell me about a problem you fixed that was not yours to fix."
- **Traps**
  - A mistake that turns out to be someone else's, which inverts the signal.
  - A fake mistake such as "I cared too much about quality".
  - Owning the mistake but changing nothing structurally, which is confession rather than ownership.
  - Picking something so small the stakes are invisible.
- **Your story**
  - [#6 owned a mistake](#bq-5) is still a placeholder and is the highest-value gap in this file.
  - Pick a real one, ideally a bad migration or a configuration error, where you raised it yourself.
  - Write the first-hour actions and the durable fix before the loop; this question is close to guaranteed.
- 🗣 "I found it before the alert did, and I told my lead before I started fixing it, because a quiet fix would have meant nobody else learned anything."

<a id="bq-v8"></a>

#### Value 8 — Earn trust, give trust

- **Their words**
  - "We build trust by communicating openly and transparently. We give trust easily, and we recognize that trusting each other is essential to our success."
  - "We don't let occasional failures and differences in work styles undermine our trust."
  - "We learn from failures rather than seeking to place blame, and we don't invoke rank to convince others we are right."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - How you conduct a disagreement, not whether you won it.
  - Whether you argue from evidence rather than from position or seniority.
  - Whether you extend trust by default, including delegating without hovering.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - The disagreement stated fairly, with the other person's position put at its strongest.
  - The evidence you brought: a benchmark, a prototype, an incident, a cost figure.
  - What you said would change your mind, ideally stated up front during the disagreement.
  - The resolution, and your relationship with that person afterwards.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "What would have changed your mind?"
  → "What if they had been your manager?"
  → "How did you work with them afterwards?"
- **Traps**
  - Winning because you were more senior, which the value names explicitly as a failure.
  - A story where the other person was simply wrong and had no reasonable case. Interviewers read that as an inability to steelman.
  - Vague resolution, where the disagreement just faded rather than being settled.
  - Confusing this with [Value 10](#bq-v10). This one is about *how* you disagreed. Value 10 is about what you did after losing.
- **Your story**
  - [#8 design disagreement](#bq-5): multi-agent versus deterministic steps.
  - Prepare the falsifier explicitly, because "what would have changed your mind" is the follow-up that separates candidates here.
- 🗣 "I said up front that if the deterministic version hit the same recall on the sample set, I would drop the argument. That made it a measurement instead of an opinion."

<a id="bq-v9"></a>

#### Value 9 — Take pride in your work

- **Their words**
  - "We strive for excellence in all that we do, and we take pride in our progress."
  - "We identify work that needs to be done to achieve our team goals, and we communicate those goals well to the broader organization."
  - "We take responsibility for either changing our work or changing ourselves when we don't find pride in our work."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Whether you hold a standard when nobody is checking.
  - Whether you can name something you refused to ship half-done, and what that refusal cost.
  - The communication clause is often missed: pride includes making the work legible to people outside your team.
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - The corner you could have cut, and that cutting it would have gone unnoticed.
  - Why you did not cut it, in terms of a concrete future failure rather than aesthetics.
  - What holding the standard cost in time or goodwill.
  - Evidence the standard held afterwards, such as the thing still being maintained or reused.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "How do you decide when good enough is good enough?"
  → "Has that ever made you late?"
  → "How does this square with shipping a grungy solution now?"
- **Traps**
  - Perfectionism with nothing delivered, which collides directly with [Value 2](#bq-v2).
  - Pride with no standard behind it, which is just satisfaction.
  - Be ready for the interviewer to set Value 2 against Value 9 deliberately. The resolution is the axis of reversibility.
  → A rough internal tool is cheap to fix later, so ship it rough.
  ⇒ An access-control model, an audit trail, or a data migration is expensive or impossible to fix later, so do those properly the first time.
- **Your story**
  - [#9 RBAC and audit logging](#bq-5): extensible resolution across roles, document types, and request attributes, with a full audit trail.
  - This is the right story precisely because access control is the category you cannot iterate into correctness afterwards.
- 🗣 "A rough test harness is a Tuesday. A rough permission model is a breach, so that one I did properly the first time."

<a id="bq-v10"></a>

#### Value 10 — Challenge ideas, champion execution

- **Their words**
  - "We ask why. We test the validity of ideas through rigor, research, and critical thinking."
  - "We have an obligation to create clarity for ourselves and others."
  - "We own decisions. We drive effective implementation."
- **What is actually being tested**
  - Both halves, and the second half is the one that discriminates.
  - Did you challenge the idea properly, with rigor rather than opinion?
  - Then, having lost, did you execute the agreed decision fully and without sabotage?
- **What a passing answer contains**
  - The decision you disagreed with, and the specific question you asked.
  - The rigor you applied: a prototype, a measurement, a written analysis.
  - The outcome of the challenge, and it is stronger if you lost.
  - Clear evidence you then delivered the thing you had argued against, properly.
  - What you did with your remaining doubt: usually a documented risk or a metric you watched.
- **Follow-ups to expect**
  → "You disagreed and still had to build it. How did that go?"
  → "Did it turn out you were right?"
  → "How do you disagree with a decision that has already been made?"
- **Traps**
  - Challenging and then disengaging, doing a visibly worse job on something you argued against. This is the failure the value exists to catch.
  - Never challenging anything, which fails the first half.
  - Being right and saying so repeatedly afterwards, which fails the collaboration values next door.
  - The strongest answer is one where you lost, executed well, and can say plainly whether the decision turned out fine.
- **Your story**
  - [#10 infrastructure delivery ownership](#bq-5) plus the argument that the pipeline should not be multi-agent.
  - The narrative arc to rehearse: argued against it with evidence, the decision went the other way, you built it properly, and you name honestly how it turned out.
- 🗣 "I argued against it, I lost, and I built it properly anyway. Then I wrote down the failure mode I was worried about and put a metric on it, so if I had been right we would have found out early."
