# Design a Load Balancer

Design a highly available L4/L7 load-balancing layer that distributes traffic across a changing backend fleet while protecting unhealthy and overloaded instances.

## Scope decisions to make

- Public regional load balancer; global traffic management is an extension.
- Support HTTP/gRPC at L7 and explain what L4 changes.
- Include health checking, connection draining, TLS termination, and optional affinity.

## Non-functionals to pin down

- Peak requests or connections per second and connection duration.
- Added p99 latency, throughput, and availability target.
- Backend fleet churn, zonal failure behavior, and config convergence SLA.

## The estimation that decides something

Estimate concurrent connections with Little's Law and network throughput from request/response size. This should decide process model, instance count, and whether connection state dominates CPU or memory.

## Skeleton diagram

```text
Client -> VIP/Anycast -> LB fleet -> healthy backend pool
                         |   |
                         |   `-> passive + active health signals
                         `-> discovery/config control plane
```

## The hard part

Balance quickly without sending synchronized traffic to the same backend. Define least-request/weighted algorithms, outlier detection, slow start, draining, and behavior when health signals disagree.

## Tradeoffs to defend

- L4 versus L7 visibility and overhead.
- Round robin, least requests, power-of-two choices, and consistent hashing.
- Active versus passive health checks.
- Session affinity versus even distribution and failover.

## Top failure mode

A dependency slowdown makes backends look alive while queues grow, causing retry amplification and cascading failure. Cover concurrency limits, outlier ejection, load shedding, retry budgets, and recovery hysteresis.

## Pushback ladder

1. Why does round robin fail when request cost varies?
2. A zone loses half its capacity and clients retry. How do you prevent overload?
3. Requests become stateful and must preserve ordering per customer. What changes?
