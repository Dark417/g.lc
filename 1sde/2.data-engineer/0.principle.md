# Doc 4 — What a Principal Data Engineer Actually Does

Purpose: (1) understand the altitude your interviewer operates at, so you hear what his questions really probe; (2) borrow the vocabulary of the level above you to signal trajectory — without claiming its scope.

---

## 1. The one-line definition

A principal DE **owns the data platform and its direction, not the pipelines on it.** They multiply other engineers: the paved roads, standards, and architectural decisions they produce determine how fast and safely twenty other engineers ship. Their unit of work is a _decision that many teams inherit_; an L3's unit of work is a _pipeline that consumers rely on_.

## 2. The ladder — where the lines actually are

| Dimension                | L2/L3 (you, top of band)                                   | Senior / L4                                                     | Staff / Principal                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owns                     | Pipelines end-to-end: build, run, debug, backfill          | A domain's data: several pipelines + its model, contracts, SLAs | The platform: architecture, standards, multi-year direction           |
| Ambiguity handled        | "Build ingestion for feed X" → working, monitored pipeline | "Our filings data is unreliable" → diagnosis + plan + delivery  | "Should our lakehouse strategy change?" → framing the question itself |
| Blast radius of mistakes | A dataset, its consumers                                   | A domain, quarters of roadmap                                   | The org's data trust, multi-million cost lines                        |
| Code output              | High — most of the week                                    | Medium — hardest parts, reviews                                 | Low — prototypes, paved-road reference implementations                |
| Influence tool           | Working software                                           | Design docs + reviews within domain                             | Standards, RFCs, cross-org persuasion, hiring bar                     |
| Failure they prevent     | A pipeline breaking                                        | A domain modeling itself into a corner                          | The org building 5 incompatible platforms                             |

The interview meaning: the manager said "lead engineer, independent on Databricks." He's hiring L3 who behaves like the _left two columns_ fully, with early signs of the middle column (design docs, contracts, mentoring instinct). He is not testing the right column — but he _asks from_ it.

## 3. A principal DE's actual week (so you can hear it in his questions)

- **Design reviews (the biggest block).** Reading other teams' pipeline/model designs and attacking them: grain? contract with consumers? failure modes? backfill story? cost? — _Notice: these are exactly the questions you're being asked. He's running a miniature design review on you._
- **Architecture decisions & RFCs.** Lakehouse layout, streaming vs batch posture, build-vs-buy (Databricks vs EMR, DLT vs Airflow+dbt, buy Monte Carlo vs build checks), written as decision docs with tradeoffs and a recommendation.
- **Standards & paved roads.** The ingestion framework everyone must use; naming and layer conventions; the pipeline template repo; the "how we do schema change" process (your Liquibase gate is a paved road — a principal built or blessed it).
- **Cost & capacity governance.** Owns the Databricks bill's shape: cluster policies, chargeback tags, quarterly capacity plans.
- **Incident leadership.** Not the 3am fix (that's you) — the post-incident review, the systemic guardrail, the "this class of failure can't recur" change.
- **Cross-org negotiation.** Data contracts with producing teams, SLA agreements with consumers, security/compliance alignment (in a bank: lineage, retention, entitlements evidence for auditors).
- **Hiring and leveling.** Sets the bar you're being measured against; mentors seniors.

## 4. Deliverables inventory (what "output" means at that level)

1. **Architecture/design docs & RFCs** — problem, options, tradeoff table, recommendation, migration path. The canonical principal artifact.
2. **Data platform strategy doc** — 1–3 year direction: what we standardize on, what we deprecate, sequencing.
3. **Standards** — medallion conventions, naming, SLA/SLO definitions for data (freshness, completeness, accuracy targets per tier).
4. **Data contracts** — schema + semantics + SLA between producer and consumer teams, versioned, enforced in CI (this is the org-scale version of your StructType drift test).
5. **Paved-road frameworks** — reference ingestion framework, pipeline template, quality-check library; a principal writes the skeleton once so 20 engineers don't write it 20 ways.
6. **Governance model** — UC layout, grant model, PII handling, audit story.
7. **Cost model** — chargeback/showback, policies, the quarterly "here's where the money goes" review.
8. **Post-incident reviews & guardrails** — the systemic fixes.
9. **Evaluations/POCs** — "we tested liquid clustering on our 3 biggest tables; adopt/don't, because…"
10. **People artifacts** — leveling rubrics, interview loops, mentoring.

## 5. Tools & frameworks landscape (mapped to purpose, principal's-eye view)

A principal's tool knowledge is _comparative_ — they know each category's 2–3 players and the deciding variable, because their job is choosing and standardizing. Learn the map, not the tools:

| Category                 | Players                                                                     | Deciding variable                                                                      | Your stack's answer                                                |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Compute/engine           | Databricks (Spark+Photon), EMR, Snowflake, BigQuery                         | DX + governance vs control vs SQL-warehouse purity                                     | Databricks + Glue                                                  |
| Table format             | Delta, Iceberg, Hudi                                                        | Ecosystem lock-in vs multi-engine neutrality (Iceberg's pitch); converging via UniForm | Delta                                                              |
| Orchestration            | Airflow, Dagster, Databricks Workflows, Prefect                             | Cross-system DAGs & org standard vs native simplicity                                  | Workflows + SQS-triggered Glue                                     |
| Transformation framework | dbt (SQL-first, tests, docs, lineage), DLT, Spark code                      | Analyst-accessible SQL layers vs engineer-owned code                                   | PySpark/Glue; DLT in your lakehouse                                |
| Ingestion/CDC            | Auto Loader, Kafka + Connect, Debezium (CDC), Fivetran (buy)                | Build vs buy; file-drop vs event-stream vs DB-log sources                              | Auto Loader / SQS+Glue                                             |
| Streaming backbone       | Kafka, Kinesis, Pulsar                                                      | Ecosystem + ops ownership                                                              | (conceptual only — say so honestly)                                |
| Data quality             | DLT expectations, Great Expectations/Soda, Monte Carlo (observability, buy) | Checks-as-code vs ML-driven anomaly detection vs vendor                                | Expectations + reconciliation checks                               |
| Catalog/governance       | Unity Catalog; enterprise: Collibra/Alation                                 | Technical enforcement vs business glossary/compliance workflow (banks often run both)  | UC                                                                 |
| Contracts/schemas        | JSON Schema/Avro + registry, contract-testing in CI                         | Where enforcement lives (CI vs runtime)                                                | Liquibase + StructType drift test — your genuine miniature of this |
| IaC/CI                   | Terraform, Databricks Asset Bundles, Jenkins/GitHub Actions                 | Org standard                                                                           | Terraform + Jenkins/Spinnaker                                      |
| Observability/FinOps     | system tables, Datadog, cost dashboards                                     | —                                                                                      | Datadog/CloudWatch + system.billing                                |

Two honest lines to keep ready: "Kafka I know conceptually, not operationally — our event backbone was SQS/SNS, which I ran in production." · "I haven't used dbt; I understand where it sits — SQL-first transformation with testing and lineage — and our DLT/PySpark layer occupies that slot."

## 6. Using this in the interview

**Hear the altitude behind the question.** "How would you lay out this table?" is a design review probe → answer with deciding variables, then a pick (that's the principal's own format). "What happens when the job fails?" is an operational-independence probe → answer with a procedure. "Why medallion?" is a standards probe → defend each layer _and_ say when you'd collapse them.

**Trajectory signals you can honestly claim (use ~2, not all):**

- "I've started pushing verification into mechanical gates — Liquibase drift tests, coverage floors — because rules in a wiki don't survive contact with a team." (paved-road instinct)
- "Before building, I write a one-page design: problem, non-goals, the build-vs-buy question." (RFC instinct)
- "I think about pipelines contract-first: what does the consumer get, at what freshness, and what breaks them." (data-contract instinct)
- "I watch the cost shape, not just correctness — job clusters over all-purpose, auto-stop, right-sized warehouses." (governance instinct)

**Scope honesty rule.** Every trajectory signal is "instinct demonstrated at my scope," never "I owned the platform." The sentence "an architect owned the blueprint; I led X within it" makes every other claim you make more credible — a principal interviewer values calibrated self-assessment as a _skill_, because miscalibrated engineers are the ones who create their incidents.

**One good question to ask him** (signals the right altitude without pretending to it): "What's the paved road here — is there a standard pipeline template and schema-change process, or is that still being built? I'm asking because the drift-gate discipline we ran saved us repeatedly, and I'd want to plug into whatever the equivalent is."
